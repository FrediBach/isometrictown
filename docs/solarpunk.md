# Solarpunk mode

The **Today | Solarpunk** switch in the preview toolbar draws the same place as a settled, repaired and inhabited future. The art direction is in [solarpunk/intro.md](solarpunk/intro.md) and its recipes; the first plan, all built, is [solarpunk/implementation-plan.md](solarpunk/implementation-plan.md). The bolder follow-up and its implementation status are in [solarpunk/bolder-plan.md](solarpunk/bolder-plan.md). The next proposal, [a town that produces its own future](solarpunk/next-plan.md), calls for widespread productive systems and larger changes to architecture and public space; its productive roofs and gardens, wind systems, public solar roofs and first stepped rooftop farms are now built.

**Depth** (`set.depth`, the Retrofit | Transform | Visionary selector in the Town section) says how far the future goes. `retrofit` is everything in the first plan, and its golden drawings must not change. `transform`, the default, also reshapes streets and plants a canopy. `visionary` adds district landmarks and new transit (see 16 below), and turns every car park into a park. Code asks `deeper(set, 'transform')`; catalogue entries name the least depth they need as a gate, `depth:transform`.

## Rules the code keeps

- **Same place.** `classify()` runs as always and its `Info` is never changed. Source footprints, heights and roof shapes remain the record of today. Water and street routes stay in place; Visionary can add stepped growing storeys above suitable buildings. At depth Retrofit the mode only adds marks and swaps a few; from Transform it also changes how a carriageway is shared out, never where it runs.
- **Off means unchanged.** With `solarpunk` off, nothing reads a plan, so the drawing is identical whatever `future` and `climate` say (`test/render/future.test.ts`). Every hook checks `B.future` first.
- **One future at every scale.** `planFuture()` never looks at the camera. Level of detail decides only how much of the plan shows.
- **Fixed to the map.** Decisions hash the building's seed (`seedFor(set, info.seed)`, so a locked building rerolls its future too) or its position, with salts from 12000 (reshaping and the canopy from 12300). LOD fades use salts from 9200. Reshaping looks for junctions among `town.nearby.ways`, the 250 m context, so a street near the edge of the view finds the same neighbours from every view.

## How it fits the pipeline

1. **Plan** (`src/future.ts`). In `render()`, straight after `classify()` and before context buildings are dropped, `planFuture()` gives every building a `Building.future`: its recipe (`B01` house, `B04` slab…), and whether it gets roof panels, a green roof or a rain tank. Rows decide together through their shared seed, so their panels line up. Landmarks, churches, castles and hospitals keep their roofs. The district plan (`FutureTown`, for now just the climate) goes on `B.future`.
2. **Climate.** `climateOf()` picks one of seven profiles from the style and the centre (`hot_dry` for the arid style, `mountain` for the Alpine one, and so on). The Town section can override it. `CLIMATES` holds what each profile asks for, such as how many roofs get planting or how many houses a tank.
3. **Draw.** Building code reads `b.future` while it builds:
   - `house()`: `roofPanels()` puts one panel group on the sun-facing slope, clear of dormers, chimneys and party walls.
   - `flat()`: plant rooms go up first (`rooftop(…, taken)`), then `futureRoof()` adds rows of panels with aisles and low planting in the room left.
   - `oneWay()`: `sawtoothPanels()` covers the opaque tooth slopes.
   - Each records its wall height in `b.future.eave`.
   - Rain tanks are an `Extra` (`commons.ts`), placed once every footprint is in `M.foot`, beside houses that were built.
4. **Streets** (M2). `traffic()` swaps some private cars for bicycles and cargo bikes (`vehicles.ts` `bicycle()`) and leaves some out, deciding per car by position with salt 12100. Fewer kerb stretches keep parking (`KERB_FREED`); `freedKerbs()` walks the same kerb slots and returns the freed ones, which become trees in pits (`kerbTrees()`, added to the tree list) or rain gardens (`kerbGardens()`, ground marks). Car park bays come from `parkingBays()` (shared with `parking()`); `bayUse()` makes some racks or beds (`lotCommons()`), and `lotCanopies()` roofs straight rows of bays. `fuel()`, `busStops()` and `platforms()` add chargers, planted roofs and panels while `B.future` is set; `streets.ts`, `trains.ts` and `power.ts` electrify every main line and bury town wires.
5. **Additions** (M3). The planner also picks one addition per building (`Future.addition`) and the wall it stands on (`side`): a balcony frame for slabs, rows of three storeys or more and perimeter blocks (their back wall), a porch for houses, an entrance canopy for civic buildings, fins for towers, a trellis for works. `Obstacles` tests the ground in front of the wall in world metres (other buildings, roads, railways, water), so the choice is the same from every view. The best frame in each 200 m cell (`B.cell`) is the district's `wonder`. `building()` calls `additions()` (`additions.ts`) after the body; `house()` records its walls in `future.wall`, so a porch finds the door the facade drew. What an addition stands on goes into `future.strip`, and `render()` fills it into `M.foot`. Squares get `publicRoom()` (a pergola and stalls, `sites.ts`); schools get `yardPergolas()`.
6. **Landscape** (M4, `landscape.ts`). Park hatching (`parkGrass()`) leaves out `meadowPatches()`, smooth noise fixed to the map, which `parkMeadow()` fills with tufts. `reeds()` puts fanned clusters on soft banks (park, meadow, marsh, garden or open country, never within 3 m of a road) and across marshes. `gardenBeds()` lays three beds behind some houses on garden ground; `worksHedges()` plants bushes along the real (non-seam) edges of industrial land. In `ground.ts`, `field()` hands wide fields to `strips()` (two to four crops with grass margins), `orchardTrees()` mixes ages and leaves gaps, and in `gardens.ts` `hedgerows()` keeps more hedges.
7. **Life** (M5). Squares and school pergolas record gathering spots on `B.future.gather` as they are made; `people()` draws figures there (children smaller, kept off roads), and squares and pedestrian streets are a little busier. Balcony frames hang washing on a few bays when the building shows close-scale life (`motifBudget()` 3, rule `washing`). Each climate's `dry` flag turns kerb rain gardens into dry stone channels.
8. **Finishing touches.** Painted cycle lanes along the kerbs of wide main roads (`markings()`), cargo bikes outside some shop bays (`shopfront()`), station forecourt canopies (`forecourtCanopies()`, kept clear of trees), paddock fences and troughs across big meadows (`paddocks()` in `ground.ts`), rain tanks and trellises by allotment sheds, shore-power cabinets by port cranes, planted medians on wide two-way primary roads (`hasMedian()` in `future.ts`, which `lanes()` in `traffic.ts` and `markings()` also read, so traffic keeps clear and the centre line goes), and park benches and rain-garden dips (`parkCommons()`).
9. **Showpieces.** `showpiece()` picks a few schools, stations and squares by a 200 m cell: they get a long learning arcade, a large forecourt canopy on branching posts, or a long pergola with climbers, falling back to the ordinary size where there is no room. School pergolas keep off mapped footpaths.
10. **Budget.** `motifBudget()` says how much of a building's future shows: the main change alone, with its supporting change (rules `futureTwo`), or with close-scale fittings (`futureDetail`).
11. **Reshaped streets** (depth Transform, `reshape.ts`). In `render()`, after the context buildings go and before any mask is filled, `reshapeTown()` returns the town with reshaped streets swapped for copies: `future` says what the street became and `was` keeps its old width. Minor streets at least 7 m wide on built-up land (`urbanity()` over 0.3) narrow to one shared 3.6 m lane; primary and secondary roads at least 12 m wide become boulevards, one lane each way either side of a planted median (`hasMedian()` is always true for them), then a 0.9 m planted buffer and a 1.8 m cycle track each side. Walking each centre line a metre at a time, strips stop 6 m short of any other road or railway, so corners stay paved, and a street is narrowed only where a third of its length or more can be planted (tram streets and short links stay as they are). Pieces of one street cut at a tile edge run on without a cap. The strips (`Reshaped.strips`) fill the `green` mask and their own `strip` mask: park grass and park trees keep off them, `stripGrass()` hatches them twice as densely as parks and edges them, `stripTrees()` plants a row of young and mature trees down each garden-street strip, spaced by the climate's `streetTrees`, and `cycleTracks()` draws the tracks' kerbs, a dashed centre line and riders. Because only widths change, kerbs, traffic lanes and markings follow; reshaped streets keep no kerb parking, and markings and pavement trees read the old width (`was`).
12. **Roofscape** (depth Transform, `solar.ts`). Panels are a dark field: `tone()` hatches each panel quad in `SHADOWS` at `cam.tone(0.7)`, shown only on the panel's own face, leaving out lines too short to read, and the module grid moves to `LINEWORK` over it, every four modules where that reads (denser, the two close up into solid ink). More pitched roofs get panels, and panels on a row run up to its party walls, so the row reads as one band. `planFuture()` gives big, low flat roofs (over 400 m², at most 30 m tall, not works or heritage) a `roofGarden`: the panels take one half of the roof along its length and `roofGarden()` plants the other with hatched planting, planters along the edge and small trees in tubs.
13. **Car parks, walls and frames** (depth Transform). `lotParks()` in `reshape.ts` groups car park aisles that meet into car parks, among the 250 m context, and turns most of them into pocket parks, each decided whole by its first end (all of them at Visionary). Their aisles leave the roads and stay as footpaths; the ground the bays would take goes into `Reshaped.lots`, filled into `green`, `strip` and a mask of its own, hatched like a park and edged by `stripGrass()`, with a grove from `lotTrees()`. `pocketCommons()` (`commons.ts`) adds pairs of food beds and path-facing benches at intervals along the retained aisles where their whole footprint fits clear of paths, trees, water and buildings. These use Parks and the existing bed/bench LOD rules; ordinary park benches skip those aisles to avoid duplicates. The planner gives rows, blocks and shops up to 30 m tall with party walls, and windowless works, `greenWalls`: `building()` sets `B.greenWalls`, and `prism()` plants the blank walls (above a lower neighbour, or with a `blank` facade) with `greenWall()` in place of windows and shade hatching. Every slab now gets its balcony frame, with a second one (`side2`, ground in `strip2`) on the opposite wall where that is clear; about half the floors of every frame are planted.
14. **Water, fields and squares** (depth Transform). `waterMargins()` in `reshape.ts` lays an 8 m band along every real (non-seam) shore of rivers, lakes, ponds and canals, and both sides of streams, outside dense centres; the bands (`Reshaped.banks`) fill the `strip` mask and a mask of their own, and `stripGrass()` hatches them like parks where the ground has no other texture, with a line of trees from `bankTrees()`. In `ground.ts`, `fieldStrips()` splits wide fields into strips of about 25 m (up to eight) and `fieldTrees()` plants a tree row on every margin. `public-energy.ts` now plans roofs on explicitly mapped pedestrian squares before tree placement: branching canopies at Transform and connected, stepped bays at Visionary. `sites.ts` keeps the associated square's furniture but omits its central fountain or statue. The roofs use `panel()` and its panel tone.
16. **Visionary.** In `reshape.ts`, minor streets in the densest districts (`urbanity()` over 0.7, half of the 300 m cells) become `future: 'pedestrian'`: no kerbs (`curbs()`), no traffic, no parking, two rows of trees in the paving (`groveTrees()`) and gathering spots for `people()`. `coreMarkets()` (`commons.ts`) adds market stalls on streets at least 8 m wide, facing a continuous 3.6 m service lane and keeping off junctions and tree crowns; Life controls them. `tramChains()` finds chains of primary and secondary pieces at least 600 m long among the context, and a little over half get twin tram tracks (one down the middle where `tramPair` doesn't pass), drawn by `railways()` under the facet `new-tram`; a road with a tram has no median. `landmarks.ts` upgrades selected Transform wind sites to 72 m machines; their map-fixed placement is described below. `agrivoltaics()` (`solar.ts`) raises rows of panels over every other strip of some wide fields, and `futureRoof()` puts a glazed `pavilion()` under a pergola on some slab and tower roofs, or rows of `rooflights()` on a `marketHall`. `flat()` cuts repeated `skyGarden` bands into suitable ordinary towers over 60 m (`tower-gardens.ts`, with small trees in `buildings.ts`); `gardenBridges()` (`landmarks.ts`) spans side streets between tall buildings; `tramStops()` puts platforms with panel canopies along tram lines (their ground goes into `foot`); and waterways in culverts (`Town.culverts`) are daylighted by joining the streams before the bank margins are laid.
15. **Canopy** (depth Transform, `canopy.ts`). `canopyTrees()` puts groves of three to seven trees, big and small, on an 18 m grid fixed to the map and gathered by smooth noise, on free town ground (not buildings, roads, water, parks, woods, fields, pitches, cemeteries or allotments). Each climate's `canopy` target sets how many; groves thin by their 4.5 m spacing. Courts over 300 m² inside apartment blocks get one to three big trees.

17. **Productive properties** (depth Transform, `production.ts`). After `planFuture()` and before context buildings are dropped, `planProduction()` reserves broad solar wings on suitable flat roofs up to 60 m tall, excluding pavilions and market halls. Their footprint and access margin are kept free of plant rooms, panel rows and roof planting; `solarWing()` draws the raised plane and braced supports. Houses and terraces with enough mapped residential ground behind them get a growing house, two broad beds and a 2.6 m rainwater cistern, or a standalone cistern where the larger package cannot fit. Complete footprints clear source buildings and their additions, paths, roads, rail, water, woodland and other mapped ground uses. Conflicting neighbouring packages both decline. Reserved ground enters `foot` before trees and furniture, and replaces the older beds/tank for that property. Ground controls the gardens and cisterns; Rooftop details controls solar wings. Planning is independent of LOD. The lab reports eligible, accepted and rejected sites; these counts include hidden and off-sheet parts of the loaded drawing area.

## Layers and pens

| Layer | Holds | Pens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `solar` | panels on pitched, flat and sawtooth roofs; canopies over car park rows | outline `LINEWORK`, a sparse grid every two modules in `SHADOWS`; from Transform a tone in `SHADOWS` with the grid in `LINEWORK` |
| `planting` | green roofs, rain tanks, rain gardens and tree pits, car park beds and racks, reeds, food beds | `GREENERY`; downpipes `WATER` |
| `additions` | balcony frames, porches, canopies, fins, trellises, school pergolas | `LINEWORK`; porch boards, pergola slats and gallery shade rails `ROOFS`; climbers and the wonder's plants `GREENERY` |
| `planting` (roof gardens) | `roof-garden`: planting hatch, edge planters, trees in tubs | `GREENERY`; planters and tubs `LINEWORK` |
| `planting:strips` | garden-street strips and boulevard buffers: `strip-planting` (tone hatch, on the sheet's grid) and `strip-edges`; car parks turned into parks: `lot-planting` and `lot-edges` | `GREENERY` |
| `planting` (green walls) | `green-wall`: climbers up blank walls, on the wall's face | `GREENERY`; the planter `LINEWORK` |
| `streets:cycle-tracks` | boulevard cycle tracks and their riders | `LINEWORK` |

Strip, car park, bank, field, canopy and courtyard trees are filed under `trees` with the facets `strip-trees`, `lot-trees`, `bank-trees`, `field-trees`, `canopy-trees` and `courtyard-trees`. Planted banks hatch under `planting:strips/bank-planting`; public solar roofs are `solar:public-roofs/energy-canopy` and `solar:public-roofs/energy-arcade`.

Panels stand a little off the roof (`standoff()`), so the depth buffer hides the roof hatching under them. When rows on a flat roof are too close to tell apart (`pvRows`), the array becomes one outlined patch. Patches test their shorter side (`pvPatch`) and keep a pen-scaled inset from the roof edge, so they don't blot at coarse scales.

Solar wings are filed under `solar/solar-wing`; growing houses, productive beds, cisterns and their pipes under `planting:productive-gardens`.

Pocket-park beds and seating are filed under `parks:pocket-commons`, market stalls under `life:core-markets`. Both reserve their ground in `foot` before later extras and people are placed.

## Checking a change

`npm run lod -- --solarpunk --name sp-after --compare sp-before` draws the lab places in the mode; `--climate hot_dry` (or any profile) draws them for one climate, to compare profiles on the same places, and `--depth retrofit` (or `visionary`) at another depth. A solarpunk run also draws every case as it is today and reports three numbers against it: **new**, the share of ink filed under the catalogue's Solarpunk group (`futureShare()`); **differs**, the share of pixels that change between the two sheets at preview size, about 900 px wide and slightly blurred (`lab/thumbs.ts`), with both thumbnails side by side in the report; and plot **time** as a multiple of today's. `--compare` shows how new and differs moved. Compare with a run of the mode before your change, and with one of the default drawing (no `--solarpunk`). The golden cases `*-solarpunk` in `test/cases.ts` reuse recorded places at depth Retrofit, and `*-transform` the same places at Transform.

Visionary lab reports also compare directly with Transform, showing paired previews and the additional pixel difference, plot time and waste. Productive-property counts distinguish roof wings, gardens, cisterns, crowded sites and sites without suitable mapped residential ground.

### Rooftop wind and larger public roofs

From Transform, `planProduction()` reserves a 6 × 6 m service and rotor envelope on suitable flat roofs 6–60 m tall, alongside the solar wing. Hospitals, market halls and pavilions are excluded. A taller building within 24 m shelters a candidate; context buildings participate before clipping. `productionRoof()` keeps plant rooms, solar arrays and planting outside both reservations. `roofWind()` draws an eight-metre vertical-axis assembly with two bowed blades, a service deck and braced supports. Its `roofWind` LOD rule retains the silhouette; blade thickness and generator detail only appear when they read. These are invented structures, not mapped wind installations or an engineering assessment of wind yield.

Squares can carry branching solar roofs 20–50 m across in Transform. Visionary prefers two to five connected bays spanning 40–96 m and falls back to a branching canopy where only that fits. Public roofs are now planned in map coordinates as described below.

### Neighbourhood wind

`planWind()` in `landmarks.ts` runs before context buildings are dropped. From Transform it places 35 m machines (24 m hub, 11 m blades) on mapped fields, farmyards, industrial land and explicitly mapped meadows. Visionary upgrades selected field and meadow sites to 72 m (48 m hub, 24 m blades), falling back to the smaller machine if only that fits. These are invented energy infrastructure, not mapped turbines or an assessment of local wind conditions.

Candidate positions and rotor phases use 360 m Web Mercator cells. Neighbour priorities enforce at least 100 true metres between sites, even at high latitudes; there is no sheet-ranked cap. Full rotor envelopes plus a 6 m margin must fit one source parcel and clear roads, railways, paths, streams, water, woodland, protected-area polygons and incompatible land uses. Building footprints receive 35 m clearance, or 80 m for the larger machines. The footprint mask reserves the sweep before trees and other inferred objects. Ground controls the feature; Power lines does not change the available context or reroll the sites.

`Town.windContext` retains complete ordinary loaded-tile polygons, building footprints and routes without additional network requests. `Area.sub` preserves the source land-cover subclass: `meadow` can qualify, whereas the renderer's visually similar `grassland`, `heath` and `fell` do not. Raw tiles retain excluded green/protected polygons in `windGreen`; assembly converts them into wind obstacles. Zoom and bearing do not select different sites. `turbine` gates the blade span, while `turbineDetail` simplifies narrow shafts, nacelles and blades. The view reach includes an 80 m structure even when Height is reduced, so a mast base outside the sheet does not discard a visible rotor.

### Connected public solar roofs

`planPublicEnergy()` uses `Town.publicContext`, retained from ordinary loaded tiles before any view filtering. Raw `publicSquares` identify pedestrian transportation polygons separately from vehicle areas, piers and airfields. Complete building footprints, water, vegetation, other paved areas, streams and routes supply exclusions. This uses the existing tile requests.

Many pedestrian polygons join a square to several streets. A bounded interior search tests roof patches against the entire source shape, including holes, rather than assuming the bounding centre is usable. It prefers larger connected roofs in Visionary, then smaller ones, and finally branching canopies. Source-position hashes and canonical axes keep the result stable across pans, bearing changes, source ordering and reversed duplicate rings. Overlapping proposals both decline.

Roofs clear motor traffic, railways, bridges, water and buildings, with a 3.2 m building margin. Paths may continue beneath them, but the 1.8 m square column reservations must clear every route. Only columns enter the footprint mask. Tree crowns are kept outside the roof envelope, while low furniture and people can use the ground beneath it. Life supplies gathering spots in the clear central area.

Each connected bay has two tilted leaves around a continuous 3.6 m skylight. Alternate bays rise 2.5 m, with the highest rim at 15.5 m; branching columns and continuous beams join the whole structure. Collectors and downpipes appear close up. `publicRoof` controls the overall span, while `publicRoofRibs` reduces a distant round canopy from twelve sectors to six. Roof faces, skylights and cast shadows share the same geometry. Broadening the system beyond mapped pedestrian squares to car parks and civic forecourts remains future work.


### Stepped rooftop farms (P3)

In Visionary, `planFuture()` gives suitable ordinary apartment slabs and commercial blocks a `gardenCrown` from `garden-building.ts`. Hosts have flat roofs, heights of 12–60 m and space at least 32 × 28 m. A complete rectangle must fit clear of holes and tile seams. The original building stays intact beneath two or three 3.8 m growing storeys, each set back four metres on all sides. Long terraces carry cultivation strips; greenhouse ribs, collectors and crops appear when legible. A supported solar crown caps the rooms. The addition stays within the existing footprint and rises by less than 15 m; no street or garden ground is consumed. This is imagined architecture, not a structural assessment of the mapped building.

The crown replaces plant rooms, ordinary roof arrays, solar wings, rotors and small pavilions on its host. Planning is independent of the camera; Rooftop details gates drawing. Building culling includes its added height. Today, Retrofit and Transform keep their previous geometry. Five-metre external growing frames are now planned separately where full ground clearance permits.


### Repeated tower gardens (P3)

Visionary selects about four fifths of ordinary flat-roofed towers over 60 m for garden bands. Actual geometry requires at least 55 m of usable height, a minimum 20 m width, and a near-rectangular footprint with no holes or tile cuts. `towerGardens()` derives two to four nine-metre-high openings spaced through the tower; each has a continuous core set back four to five metres. The deck projects from that core but remains inside the mapped footprint. Complete support envelopes must fit the host, including concave boundary checks.

`flat()` builds the intervening floors, greenhouse cores and supported terraces before the depth pass. Four corner columns carry the outer corners of the floors above; broad greenhouse ribs replace ordinary windows inside each growing level. Continuous sun-fin screens are omitted on these hosts so they do not cross the openings. Cultivation strips survive distant scales; crops and small trees appear closer in. Tree crowns keep a bounded physical height, rather than stretching upwards at shallow camera angles. No new ground reservations or increased culling height are needed. Hospitals, civic landmarks, castles and places of worship are excluded. Today, Retrofit and Transform are unchanged.


### Productive apartment galleries (P3)

From Transform, `planGrowingFrames()` selects up to two opposite clear walls on ordinary flat-roofed apartment blocks 9–36 m tall, with at least 16 m of usable wall. Each gallery is five metres deep and no more than 60 m long. The planner runs after `planFuture()` and before productive-property reservations or context clipping. Full envelopes are checked against context buildings, existing additions, paths, roads, rail, water, streams and other mapped ground obstacles. Conflicting proposals both decline, independently of source order. Unknown ground beside a mapped apartment is inferred as potentially usable; no additional network data is requested.

Accepted frames replace the smaller balcony frames on their hosts. `growingFrame()` draws a terrace every two storeys, alternating food-growing bays with shared greenhouse rooms in temperate/cold climates; hot gallery climates keep open bays. A supported solar shade roof rises two metres above the host eave, with collection gutters and a downpipe when legible. Glazing faces hide competing host windows. At distant scale cultivation strips replace crowded deck outlines, and greenhouse edges share the continuous columns. Physical footprints remain independent of LOD and are reserved before trees, vehicles and private productive gardens. Building culling includes the outward extension. Today and Retrofit are unchanged; constrained hosts retain their earlier additions.


### Shared water storage (P4)

`planSharedWater()` runs before context buildings are dropped, after public solar roofs and wind sites have reserved their ground. Mapped pedestrian squares and education campuses can receive one complete 18 × 16 m water court, with a metre of clearance around it. The planner keeps buildings, productive galleries, paths, roads, rail, water, streams, playing fields, playgrounds and natural cover clear; overlapping proposals both decline. Buildings have a 5.8 m service margin. Full ordinary-tile context is retained in `Town.publicContext.waterSites` and `.waterObstacles`; generic plazas, airfields and arbitrary open land do not qualify.

Transform courts have two five-metre-diameter cisterns on plinths, a nine-metre solar rain collector and planted irrigation beds. Visionary replaces the pair with an eight-metre vessel on a twelve-metre platform, supported by four braced columns. A broad collection crown with solar facets reaches 22 m. Closed vessel faces hide their interiors; elevated vessels and decks cast face shadows rather than treating the space below as solid. Full footprints enter `M.foot` before trees and furniture, and tree crowns are kept outside. A court replaces its source square's earlier fountain/statue composition. Ground controls these courts; Today and Retrofit stay unchanged.

Productive apartment galleries also gain paired four-metre cisterns on their already reserved ground, below the first growing deck. They share a low header and a connection to the roof downpipe. `sharedCistern` retains them at close scale; the footprint remains reserved at distant scales. They are under-deck ground storage, not added roof loads. The courts and tanks depict collected rainwater and irrigation, without claims about potability, water yield or engineering suitability. Stepped water-garden basins and broader neighbourhood connections remain future work.
