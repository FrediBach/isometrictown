ISOMETRIC TOWN · FIRST ARTISTIC REVIEW · 15 NOVEMBER 2026

Make each mark earn its place.

The strongest improvement is a clearer hierarchy: confident silhouettes, selective architectural rhythm, and much quieter ground and water. The app already has a rich vocabulary. More detail is rarely what these prints need.

Implementation round 1: roof hatch endings, small cars, road dashes and kerb joins, with before/after evidence. The review below records the original baseline.

What was reviewed

Fresh renders of all 12 golden cases, all 29 bundled presets at their defined centres, bearings and scales, whole-sheet contact sheets, every preset’s centre and busiest 30 × 20 mm crop, and a 50 × 40 mm centre crop of every golden. The review also inspected the catalogue and the drawing implementations behind the main mark families. No drawing code or goldens were changed.

The preset renders use default A3 landscape settings, Detail 1, a 0.35 mm pen and the default palette, with optional OSM roof tags off for a stable comparison. This is not an exact live-browser replay of every preset: the UI can retain user settings, and its normal defaults enable tags. The Mollis default golden does use its recorded tags. Other goldens preserve their explicit test settings, including A4, portrait, title, custom layers and broad monochrome pen.

Validation: npm test passed: 533 tests in 11 files. npm run build passed, with the existing large-bundle advisory. Fresh preset renders completed 29/29. Numerical goldens are in test/golden; their current SVG sheets are in test/out.

Priority 1 = broad impact or a visible clarity problem; Priority 2 = refinement; Priority 3 = a proposed direction requiring dedicated samples. Observations and artistic preferences are distinguished from source-based hypotheses. No physical prints were inspected. Enlarged crops reveal stroke structure but do not establish physical print quality. Rare elements, every regional variant and every possible setting combination have not been individually certified.

Keep the precise isometric linework, the economical object vocabulary, the regional façade families, clear paper-colour walls, and the playfulness of boats, vehicles and titles. Avoid adding arbitrary jitter to make the drawing look “handmade”. Intention should come from selection, rhythm and composition.

Findings and proposed improvements

01 · Priority 1 · Whole sheets: Mollis, Nördlingen, Sun Lakes, Cupertino

Visual hierarchy

The scene gives nearly every roof, garden and street equal attention. The eye keeps moving without enough places to rest.

Improve: Establish three levels: subject silhouettes, supporting architecture, and sparse atmosphere. Reserve the fullest detail for recognisable landmarks and selected streets; reduce incidental marks in already busy neighbourhoods. Express hierarchy through spacing, omission and continuity, so it works with real pens.

Implementation areas: render.ts, lod.ts

02 · Priority 1 · Jumeirah Village Circle centre close-up; roof and wall junctions across the presets

Contour continuity

Several kerbs and building edges contain short breaks, nubs or closely parallel pieces. These look accidental beside the otherwise clean geometry. The images establish the symptom, not a single proven cause.

Improve: Audit mask-boundary tracing, joins and visibility tolerances. Keep one deliberate contour per visible boundary; merge only near-collinear fragments belonging to that boundary. Preserve genuine occlusions and separate features. Fix this before adding decorative detail.

Implementation areas: raster.ts, streets.ts, roofs.ts, plot.ts

03 · Priority 1 · Mollis, Lenzburg, Hallstatt, Rye and Nördlingen; roof close-ups

Roof hatching: tonal weight

Red roof hatching is often the strongest tone everywhere, including minor buildings. Large roofs become very assertive striped panels.

Improve: Introduce a small number of roof tones: quiet sunlit slopes, medium shaded slopes and occasional strong accents. Open the default spacing, particularly on large surfaces; leave deliberate unhatched areas. Keep the existing projection-aware perpendicular spacing.

Implementation areas: roofs.ts, settings.ts

04 · Priority 1 · Mollis and Nördlingen busiest crops; Venice centre

Roof edges and hatch endings

Hatching meets ridge, eave and hip lines directly. Together with nearby roof outlines, this creates heavy seams and arrow-like junctions.

Improve: Inset hatch endpoints by a nib-aware clearance, suppress strokes that end too close to a corner, and choose one owner for shared edges. Reduce doubled outlines when their separation cannot read. Preserve the roof silhouette and main ridge.

Implementation areas: roofs.ts, buildings.ts, raster.ts

05 · Priority 2 · Source review and pitched-roof sheets

Roof material vocabulary

Down-slope parallel strokes describe almost every pitched surface. Roof shapes vary more than the marks describing their material.

Improve: Develop restrained families: occasional seam lines for metal, broken courses for tile or slate, a sparse directional treatment for thatch. Use few marks per plane, selected by building/region, without turning roofs into miniature tile inventories.

Implementation areas: roofs.ts, styles.ts

06 · Priority 2 · Amsterdam preset and golden; Rye; Aubervilliers

Complex roofs and massing

Some large masses and repetitive roof structures dominate the fine-grained streets around them. In an art print, an inferred mass can become a stronger subject than the actual town pattern.

Improve: Review large compound footprints as connected wings and courtyards. Preserve their characteristic outline while simplifying secondary roof intersections. Compare alternative inferred heights and roof profiles for the most prominent blocks; do not add arbitrary subdivisions everywhere.

Implementation areas: classify.ts, shape.ts, buildings.ts, roofs.ts

07 · Priority 1 · Sapporo centre; Jumeirah whole sheet; Midtown golden

Façade reduction across scales

Coarser buildings alternate between almost blank boxes and dense floor stripes. Both are legitimate reductions, but their contrast can feel unrelated to the building character.

Improve: Keep one or two identity cues at intermediate scales: grouped bays, an entrance, a stair strip or a setback. Use a coherent reduced façade for an entire building, with selective floor bands rather than all-or-nothing detail.

Implementation areas: facades.ts, lod.ts

08 · Priority 1 · Midtown golden; Kowloon preset

Tower façades

Repeated horizontal floor lines dominate the tall buildings, making the image read as stacks of ruled slabs.

Improve: Group floors into larger rhythms, retain occasional vertical accents, and keep some faces quiet. Let silhouette, setbacks and a few façade divisions carry height. Compare the result in monochrome before tuning colour.

Implementation areas: facades.ts, buildings.ts

09 · Priority 2 · Venice and Lemmer close-ups; Mollis layers golden

Windows, shutters, doors and balconies

The opening vocabulary is already varied, but small shutters and surrounding frames sometimes close into coloured bars. Dense window grids can become wallpaper.

Improve: Retain the existing façade families. Reduce each opening as a unit: one frame or one tick before adding shutters or glazing bars. Reserve balcony rails, door hoods and awning stripes for openings with adequate clear space, and let selected entrances provide accents.

Implementation areas: facades.ts, classify.ts, buildings.ts

10 · Priority 2 · Aubervilliers busiest crop; Mollis roofs; Sapporo centre

Chimneys, dormers, parapets and rooftop equipment

Small roof details can become dark studs; large flat roofs sometimes retain only an isolated generic box.

Improve: Use a visible-detail budget per roof. Prefer one legible chimney/dormer or a coherent equipment group over several clipped fragments. Simplify parapets to one line where the double edge closes. Protect a little unhatched space around the chosen detail.

Implementation areas: buildings.ts, roofs.ts, lod.ts

11 · Priority 2 · Mollis church, Nördlingen centre, Lenzburg courts

Landmark emphasis

Landmarks are present, but the surrounding roof and ground textures often compete with them.

Improve: Give landmarks more breathing room in adjacent texture and preserve their distinctive silhouette longer than secondary details. Keep towers, spires, domes, flags and battlements as carefully chosen accents; avoid inventing several competing focal points in one area.

Implementation areas: classify.ts, buildings.ts, render.ts

12 · Priority 1 · Mollis centre; broad-pen golden; Palmanova measurements

Cast-shadow shape and fragments

Shadows help ground buildings, but small detached hatch islands and dense fringes can read as scraps. Broad monochrome shadows resemble additional structural bars.

Improve: Remove tiny shadow components as whole shapes, simplify ragged boundaries and preserve a clear connection to the casting object where appropriate. Use fewer, longer strokes in small shadows. Keep the existing union mask rather than hatching every overlapping shadow separately.

Implementation areas: render.ts, raster.ts

13 · Priority 1 · Tree/road intersections in Sapporo and Neuf-Brisach; Kowloon streets

Hatch layering

Shadow, vegetation and infrastructure can all occupy the same small patch. Each layer is reasonable alone; their combination is visually tangled.

Improve: Define local texture precedence: structure first, then one dominant tone. Reduce background hatch behind fine foreground lines, and reserve small clearances around tree trunks, rails and important outlines. Avoid a uniform halo around every object.

Implementation areas: render.ts, raster.ts, ground.ts

14 · Priority 1 · mollis-broad-pen golden, 0.7 mm, Detail 0.5

Monochrome and broad pens

Removing colour exposes competition between shadow strokes, roof strokes and contours. Fine marginal lettering also becomes much heavier than the place title.

Improve: Treat mono as a composed drawing mode: fewer tonal strokes, more white space, simpler roof seams and fewer coordinate labels. Make the structural silhouette readable before introducing shadows. Keep physical pen widths honest; do not solve this with screen-only opacity.

Implementation areas: render.ts, settings.ts, marginalia.ts, lod.ts

15 · Priority 1 · Wädenswil, Hallstatt, Venice, Lemmer

Water texture

The repeated short sine-wave strokes form a nearly uniform blue carpet. Large water areas lose their potential as calm negative space.

Improve: Draw waves in loose bands with substantial blank water between them. Use longer, flatter strokes for lakes/canals and a restrained separate vocabulary for open water. Vary density at patch scale, not by adding independent noise to each wave.

Implementation areas: render.ts

16 · Priority 2 · Hallstatt, Lemmer, Sułoszowa; source review of pools

Shorelines, streams and pools

Water texture often approaches the bank closely, so the shoreline competes with its interior. Small water features need a distinct scale of mark.

Improve: Reserve a narrow quiet band along selected banks and around moorings. Keep streams as elegant continuous lines when narrow; avoid miniature lake texture inside them. Retain the distinct straight pool ripples, but simplify them before they crowd the pool outline.

Implementation areas: render.ts, streets.ts, tiles.ts

17 · Priority 1 · Hallstatt woods; Sapporo and Kowloon close-ups

Tree silhouettes

Circles with inner arcs and triangular conifers are charming individually, but repeated at forest scale they read as a sheet of symbols.

Improve: Keep the economical character but add a few asymmetrical crown silhouettes, varied trunk offsets and two or three conifer profiles. Use one interior branch/crown mark only when it describes form. Avoid per-vertex jitter.

Implementation areas: nature.ts

18 · Priority 1 · Emmental golden; Hallstatt; Palmanova; Neuf-Brisach

Woodland composition

Woods have a broadly even distribution of individual tree symbols. Large areas become busy without gaining much depth.

Improve: Place groves and clearings using stable world-space patches. Emphasise woodland edges and a few foreground crowns; simplify interiors into sparse grouped canopy contours. Preserve meaningful gaps around buildings, paths and water.

Implementation areas: nature.ts, ground.ts, render.ts

19 · Priority 1 · Mollis, Bauma, Pointe-Noire; golden centre crops

Meadow, lawn and open-country marks

Repeated small W-shaped tufts fill almost every available grassy gap. Many do not add information or composition.

Improve: Use sparse clusters, leaving most lawn interiors blank. Place tufts near edges, changes in land character and a few open patches. Let scale and neighbourhood density control the number of clusters, with deliberate variation in cluster size.

Implementation areas: ground.ts

20 · Priority 1 · Bourtange, Palmanova, Neuf-Brisach, Celebration

Park hatching

Continuous diagonal park hatching covers large areas and often combines with trees and their shadows. It can outweigh the town it surrounds.

Improve: Use lighter park tone and quiet zones near paths and buildings. Distinguish open lawn from woodland with different density, rather than adding the same base hatch under both. Retain the existing lighter treatment beneath pitches and graves.

Implementation areas: render.ts, ground.ts

21 · Priority 2 · Sułoszowa; Bauma; Emmental

Fields and stubble

Long furrows beautifully reveal land divisions, but uninterrupted full-field stripes become an overpowering screen, particularly in Sułoszowa.

Improve: Keep the long directional rhythm; add restrained headlands and deliberate blank breaks. Give adjacent fields a small set of coherent tones. Use stubble in patches and vary row-group length without randomising every dash.

Implementation areas: ground.ts

22 · Priority 2 · Source review; agricultural areas in the sheets

Orchards, vineyards, allotments and hedgerows

These systems use useful regular spacing, but their repeated dots, posts and plot grids can merge with general ground detail.

Improve: Preserve row rhythm with fewer complete specimens and clearer lanes between groups. Use hedgerows as flowing boundaries with occasional taller crowns. At small scales retain the planting pattern, not every tree or stake. Test the orchard dot fallback explicitly, because its nominal mark is smaller than the normal speck cutoff.

Implementation areas: ground.ts, gardens.ts, raster.ts

23 · Priority 2 · Jumeirah measurements; source review of dot textures

Sand, beach and dry-ground stipple

The lab flags 97.7% waste for Jumeirah’s ground layer. This is not proof that the texture is wrong: intentional dots are counted as specks by the metric.

Improve: Review the dot pattern as tone, separately from accidental specks. Prefer sparse clusters and blank sand, with lighter texture around parasols and towels. Measure intentional stipple separately so useful dots are not removed merely to improve the score.

Implementation areas: ground.ts, lab/metrics.ts

24 · Priority 3 · Source review; insufficient close-up evidence for a visual verdict

Marsh, rock, ice and quarry marks

The code supplies distinct symbols and nested quarry contours, but this review does not establish how every rare surface looks in a representative print.

Improve: Create dedicated samples: reeds along wet edges, rock marks grouped into outcrops, crevasses following a coherent direction, and a few readable quarry benches. Keep broad areas quiet. These are proposed treatments, not confirmed defects in the reviewed sheets.

Implementation areas: ground.ts, sites.ts

25 · Priority 2 · Mollis, Celebration, Sun Lakes; source review

Garden boundaries and small domestic objects

Fences, hedges, driveways, sheds and garden texture can fill the spaces that would otherwise separate buildings.

Improve: Choose a dominant garden cue per plot: a boundary, a tree or a shed. Omit subordinate fence segments and tufts in small plots. Align details with the plot’s geometry and preserve an open foreground strip where it helps the house read.

Implementation areas: gardens.ts, ground.ts

26 · Priority 1 · Amsterdam busiest crop; Bourtange parking; Cupertino streets

Cars, parking and small vehicle detail

Small vehicles often appear as heavy black bars with stacked interior strokes. Parking arrays add strong mechanical repetition.

Improve: Provide a genuinely simpler middle-scale car: body outline plus one cabin cue. Drop the interior before dropping the vehicle. Use a few coherent occupied groups and empty bays; keep parked cars clear of road furniture and background hatching in projection.

Implementation areas: buildings.ts, traffic.ts, infra.ts, vehicles.ts

27 · Priority 1 · Sapporo markings: 69.5% measured waste; source review

Road markings and crossings

Markings are gated by nominal size, but a short ground dash can project into an unhelpfully small or crowded mark.

Improve: Gate dashes and stripes by their actual projected length and separation. Reduce markings where cars, junction contours and shadows already describe the road. Keep only a few legible crossings at intermediate scales. Confirm the affected Sapporo marks in a layer-isolated proof before changing them globally.

Implementation areas: infra.ts, lod.ts

28 · Priority 1 · Cupertino paths: 44.9% measured waste; Bourtange: 33.2%

Footpaths, steps and wheel ruts

Many small dashed fragments cost visual attention without providing a clear route. Dense path networks add another texture to parks.

Improve: Preserve route continuity with longer, better-spaced dashes; omit short isolated runs. Simplify steps as a group and merge twin ruts when their projected separation closes. Clear a little background texture along important paths.

Implementation areas: streets.ts, lod.ts

29 · Priority 1 · Celebration and Cupertino centre crops; Neuf-Brisach woods

Power and overhead wires

Long black wires cross roofs and vegetation as a prominent extra graphic layer. They can distract from more meaningful forms.

Improve: Default invented wires to a quieter, selectively placed treatment. Keep one readable catenary per corridor at intermediate scales, with occasional support poles. Avoid long invented runs across a landmark composition; retain the existing world anchoring and title clearance.

Implementation areas: power.ts, streets.ts, infra.ts

30 · Priority 2 · Wädenswil waterfront; Amsterdam crossings; airport golden

Railways, bridges and transport structures

Rails, overhead lines, bridge railings and nearby traffic stack into dense linear corridors.

Improve: Choose the main rail or bridge silhouette first, then a sparse structural rhythm. Simplify sleepers, balusters and catenary together by projected spacing. Preserve a clear bridge opening or arch and reduce water/shadow marks immediately behind it. Platforms, portals, barriers and gantries should follow the same rule.

Implementation areas: streets.ts, infra.ts, trains.ts

31 · Priority 2 · Lenzburg centre; Mollis; source review

Street furniture, fountains and squares

Open squares are valuable pauses, but small furniture and invented focal objects can become dark knots or fill that pause unnecessarily.

Improve: Keep the square mostly empty. Use one clear fountain or statue and a few well-separated lamps or figures. Simplify fountains to basin plus one water gesture. For shelters and fuel canopies, retain roof and supports before minor signs and pump details.

Implementation areas: sites.ts, streets.ts

32 · Priority 2 · Hallstatt, Wädenswil, Lemmer; source review of ships

Boats, ships, wakes and ferry routes

Boats can be welcome accents, but the strong surrounding wave field weakens them. A dotted ferry route reads as cartographic notation.

Improve: Retain and extend the existing calm-water masks around selected vessels. Use a recognisable hull/sail and one wake gesture before fine deck detail. Make ferry route dots optional in an art-oriented treatment; keep the boat as the narrative cue. Large ship families need dedicated visual samples before detailed redesign.

Implementation areas: ships.ts, infra.ts, render.ts

33 · Priority 2 · Zürich Airport golden

Airfields and aircraft

Aircraft silhouettes read well, but apron edges, taxiways and repeated planes create a dense diagram in some areas.

Improve: Preserve the aircraft silhouettes and broad open apron. Select a few strongly legible aircraft groups, simplify small internal aircraft marks, and suppress tertiary apron/ground texture. Keep runway and taxiway hierarchy clearer than ancillary road detail.

Implementation areas: aircraft.ts, infra.ts

34 · Priority 3 · Source review; fine-scale visibility is limited in the selected defaults

People, livestock and working scenes

The app has considerable narrative detail, but many defaults are too coarse to judge figures, animals and machinery fairly.

Improve: Use small composed scenes: a pair of people, a herd edge, a tractor with a clear swath. Reserve surrounding space. At fine scales inspect pose and silhouette; at coarser scales omit the whole unreadable gesture. Do not increase population just to make the feature visible.

Implementation areas: sites.ts, ground.ts, sport.ts, vehicles.ts

35 · Priority 3 · Source review; small pitch marks visible in Mollis and parks

Sports, playgrounds and campsites

Repeated equipment and tiny actors risk becoming a catalogue of marks rather than a readable scene; the rare features need dedicated proofs.

Improve: Let pitch or track geometry carry the scene, with a few deliberately positioned players. Keep a swing or tent silhouette complete before adding parts. Use sparse coherent groups for campsites and racing, with enough white space to read the action.

Implementation areas: sport.ts, sites.ts, ground.ts

36 · Priority 3 · Source review; industrial roofs visible in several presets

Ports, industrial details and farmyards

Cranes, containers, silos, bales and machinery have different useful silhouettes, but many individual examples are not visible clearly enough here for a defect claim.

Improve: Build a sample sheet at their intended print sizes. Retain boom, hook, barrel or bale silhouette; omit repeated ribs, braces and small wheels first. Group containers and bales into masses with a few subdivisions. Keep farm-work marks aligned with the field.

Implementation areas: sites.ts, vehicles.ts, ground.ts

37 · Priority 2 · Shirakawa-go, Nantucket, Pointe-Noire, Venice

Regional artistic identity

The plans differ strongly, but the same red roof stripes, W tufts and circle/triangle trees make distant regions feel more alike than they need to.

Improve: Extend the existing regional families with a few decisive silhouette and mark choices. Prioritise steep rural roofs in Shirakawa-go, restrained coastal roof/woodland character in Nantucket, and broader tropical vegetation forms in Pointe-Noire. Treat these as art direction experiments, checked against the actual local forms before adoption.

Implementation areas: styles.ts, classify.ts, nature.ts, roofs.ts

38 · Priority 2 · All default-colour sheets

Palette as emphasis

The red/green/blue separation is immediately recognisable, but every material competes at high saturation. Pale shadows work in colour and become much stronger in mono.

Improve: Offer a restrained print palette with one accent colour and quieter supporting inks. Keep the current lively palette as a choice. Judge the tonal hierarchy in one ink first; colours should reinforce it. Preserve recolouring without rerendering.

Implementation areas: palettes.ts, settings.ts, plot.ts

39 · Priority 2 · All sheets; broad-pen golden

Frame and marginalia

The double border and frequent coordinates make a conspicuously cartographic frame. With a broad pen, small technical text becomes dense.

Improve: Offer an art-print margin: a single quiet frame or no inner border, larger breathing room, place title and a short imprint. Keep OSM attribution. Make coordinate ticks and technical notes optional as a group; adapt text size and spacing to pen width.

Implementation areas: marginalia.ts, lettering.ts

40 · Priority 2 · mollis-title golden

Cinematic title

The title is a successful graphic idea, but this placement crowds the upper edge and introduces a second strong subject into an already busy scene.

Improve: Choose a title-specific composition with generous clearance, a quieter area behind the letters and a clearly legible face. Simplify nearby tone before increasing reveal or size. Treat title prints as a separate composition family, not an overlay that must work on every crop.

Implementation areas: title.ts, render.ts

41 · Priority 1 · Cupertino, Hallstatt, La Plata; all 29 reviewed below

Preset composition

A shared default treatment cannot make every crop an intentional print. Cupertino cuts off its ring subject; La Plata becomes a fine grid; Hallstatt’s woods cannot convey its steep terrain.

Improve: Curate centre, bearing, scale and a small number of artistic settings per preset. Give each a stated subject: town plan, skyline, waterfront or village scene. Keep the current flat-world premise unless a separate terrain project is desired; choose framing that works within it.

Implementation areas: app/model.ts, settings.ts

42 · Priority 1 · Dense roofs, woods and street corridors across the review

Local density and projected clearance

Individual LOD gates do not ensure that several surviving features remain readable together. This is the common cause of much visual competition.

Improve: Add a measured local budget for optional marks in sheet space, with priority given to silhouettes and characteristic details. Suppress whole low-priority motifs rather than shaving all strokes indiscriminately. Keep object placement and identity deterministic in world coordinates.

Implementation areas: lod.ts, render.ts, raster.ts

43 · Priority 2 · 12 goldens versus 29 presets; golden and preset Amsterdam/Hallstatt use different centres

Artistic regression coverage

The tests confirm deterministic output, not artistic quality. The golden set misses many preset compositions and does not prove every rare element looks good.

Improve: Add a small curated visual proof set: mono, broad pen, dense roof junction, forest edge, open water, tower façade, coarse city plan and rare-feature swatches. Review at physical size and as a whole sheet. Keep changed-golden approval separate from the aesthetic judgement.

Implementation areas: test/cases.ts, lab/places.ts, lab/metrics.ts

For each round, compare before/after whole sheets and the same close-ups at the same settings, then inspect the changed golden counts and LOD metrics. Approve a mark because it strengthens silhouette, material, depth, rhythm or story—not merely because it is available to draw. A useful detail may be worth its plotting time; an unreadable one is not.

Acceptance questions: Does the subject read at arm’s length? Do contours remain confident close up? Can neighbouring forms be separated in one ink? Does every texture have quiet space? Are small motifs still recognisable at physical size? Has a lower waste score actually produced a better print?
